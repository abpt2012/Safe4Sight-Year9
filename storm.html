Storm Readiness Checklist

Myanmar's cyclone seasons usually include a pre-monsoon period from mid-April to May and a post-monsoon period in October and November. Storms are very frequent and destructive during these time periods, so prepare early and follow warnings to stay safe.

Recent Severe Weather

2026
Monsoon Flooding August

Over 430 deaths; catastrophic inland flooding and landslides across central and eastern Myanmar.

Event Overview & Impact

Between late July and mid-August 2026, continuous monsoon rainfall triggered widespread flooding and landslides across 41 townships in central, lower, and western Myanmar—threatening up to 1.3 million people.

Impact

Overflowing rivers—including the Ngawun, Sittaung, Irrawaddy, and Thanlwin submerged thousands of homes, schools, roads, and farmlands. Over 63,000 people in Ayeyarwady Region alone (across Laymyethna, Hinthada, Yekyi, and Kyangin) and thousands more in Kayin State were forced into temporary flood relief camps, with dozens of casualties and missing persons reported nationwide.

Why It Happened

A persistent, pronounced monsoon trough stalled over Myanmar for weeks, dumping relentless rain onto saturated river basins and swelling major river systems past their danger levels.

What should've been prepared

River-Level Early AlertsExpanding automated river-gauge warnings and local loudspeaker alerts for low-lying wards along the Ngawun, Thanlwin, and Irrawaddy rivers.

Elevated Relief Camps & Boats Pre-designating flood-safe schools and monasteries on high ground, stocked with rescue boats, clean drinking water, and sanitation kits.

Agricultural & Livelihood Protection Building elevated grain storage and drainage embankments to protect rice paddies and livestock from seasonal river overflow.

2025
Cyclone Wipha Floods July

Started in Rakhine and slowly impacted the rest of Myanmar in May. Strong winds, heavy rain and a damaging storm surge caused flooding in parts of Rakhine, including Sittwe.

Event Overview & Impact

In late July 2025, the westward-moving remnants of Severe Tropical Storm Wipha swept across Myanmar after crossing the South China Sea, supercharging the southwest monsoon across eastern Bago, Kayin, Mon, and southern Shan States.

Impact

The storm killed at least 16 people including victims of landslides in Shan State, flooding in Taungoo, and a capsized boat on the Yangon River. Over 85,000 people were affected in Bago's Taungoo District (where rising Sittaung River waters breached a dam near Madauk), while more than 15,000 people were displaced in Hpa-an, Kayin State, and hundreds of homes and schools were destroyed by high winds and floods in Mon State.

Why It Happened

Even after weakening over land in Vietnam and Laos, Wipha's low-pressure remnants tracked west into the Bay of Bengal, pulling heavy moisture inland and triggering extreme rainfall over eastern hills and the Sittaung River basin.

What should've been prepared

Dam & Embankment Monitoring Inspecting and reinforcing aging river embankments and small dams along the Sittaung River before peak monsoon storms arrive.

River & Marine Travel Bans Strictly suspending small-boat river crossings in Yangon and coastal estuaries during storm-force wind advisories.

Disease Prevention in Camps Rapidly deploying clean water, mosquito nets, and hygiene supplies to overcrowded relief shelters to prevent post-flood outbreaks of diarrhea and dengue.

Past History

2024
Typhoon Yagi (Remnants)

Over 430 deaths; catastrophic inland flooding and landslides across central and eastern Myanmar.

Event Overview & Impact

In September 2024, the remnants of Super Typhoon Yagi swept inland across central and eastern Myanmar, unleashing days of torrential monsoon rain. The resulting flash floods and landslides devastated communities far from the coast, affecting over one million people.

Impact

The floods claimed over 430 lives, left dozens missing, and submerged hundreds of thousands of homes, schools, and farmlands across Nay Pyi Taw, Mandalay, Shan, Kayah, and Bago, destroying vital rice crops and washing away roads and bridges.

Why It Happened

After making landfall in Vietnam as a super typhoon, Yagi's remnant low-pressure system interacted with an active southwest monsoon trough over Myanmar, dumping extreme, continuous rainfall onto already saturated river basins and steep mountain slopes.

What should've been prepared

Inland Flood WarningsExtending severe storm alerts and early evacuation orders to inland river valleys and highland towns not just coastal areas.

High-Ground Evacuation Routes Mapping clear, accessible upland shelters for low-lying villages near rivers and dams before monsoon peaks.

Waterproof Go-Bags & Boat Access Pre-positioning rescue boats, life jackets, and sealed dry rations in flood-prone townships.

2023
Cyclone Mocha

Started in Rakhine and slowly impacted the rest of Myanmar in May. Strong winds, heavy rain and a damaging storm surge caused flooding in parts of Rakhine, including Sittwe.

Event Overview & Impact

On May 14, 2023, Extremely Severe Cyclonic Storm Mocha made landfall near Sittwe in Rakhine State with winds exceeding 200 km/h, before tracking inland across Chin, Magway, and Sagaing. It was one of the strongest cyclones ever recorded in the North Indian Ocean.

Impact

The storm killed at least 145 people (with higher local estimates), injured hundreds, and destroyed over 200,000 homes, schools, hospitals, and displacement camps across Rakhine and northwestern Myanmar, while severing power and telecom networks for weeks.

Why It Happened

Unusually warm sea surface temperatures in the Bay of Bengal fueled rapid intensification just before landfall, pushing a 3 to 3.5-meter storm surge directly into low-lying coastal towns and estuaries around Sittwe.

What should've been prepared

Mandatory Coastal Evacuation Moving residents and vulnerable camp populations away from low-lying surge zones at least 48 hours before landfall.

Cyclone-Resistant Shelters Building elevated, reinforced concrete multi-purpose shelters capable of withstanding Category 5 winds and storm surges.

Backup Communications & Water Stockpiling solar chargers, satellite/radio links, and clean drinking water before saltwater contaminates local wells.

2010
Cyclone Giri

Crossed the Rakhine coast in October, affecting communities including areas around Kyaukphyu.

Event Overview & Impact

On October 22, 2010, Cyclone Giri rapidly intensified into a Category 4-equivalent storm and struck the Rakhine coast near Kyaukphyu, bringing violent winds and a massive storm surge to central Rakhine communities.

Impact

Giri killed 157 people, destroyed over 20,000 homes, and left more than 100,000 people homeless across Kyaukphyu, Myebon, Pauktaw, and Minbya, while wiping out fishing boats, paddy fields, and salt farms.

Why It Happened

Favorable upper-level winds and warm Bay of Bengal waters allowed Giri to undergo explosive intensification within 24 hours of hitting the Rakhine coastline, giving coastal villages very little time to react.

What should've been prepared

Rapid Last-Mile AlertsUsing sirens, mosque/monastery loudspeakers, and mobile broadcasts to warn remote coastal and island villages of sudden storm intensification.

Wind-Secure Roofing Reinforcing timber homes with hurricane straps and storm shutters to prevent roofs from tearing off in extreme gusts.

Livelihood & Seed Reserves Storing emergency grain, seed banks, and boat-mooring anchors so fishing and farming communities can recover quickly.

2008
Cyclone Nargis

Affected the Ayeyarwady region in early May and caused catastrophic damage and loss of life across the delta.

Event Overview & Impact

On May 2–3, 2008, Cyclone Nargis struck the low-lying Ayeyarwady Delta and Yangon, becoming the deadliest natural disaster in Myanmar's recorded history and devastating the nation's primary rice-producing region.

Impact

Nargis killed over 138,000 people, left 2.4 million people severely affected, and wiped out entire delta villages as a 3.5 to 4-meter storm surge swept miles inland destroying homes, schools, livestock, and critical agricultural land.

Why It Happened

Instead of curving north toward Bangladesh as Bay of Bengal cyclones usually do, Nargis tracked east directly into the flat, funnel-shaped Ayeyarwady Delta, where widespread mangrove deforestation left coastal communities defenseless against the storm surge.

What should've been prepared

Mangrove Restoration Protecting and replanting coastal mangrove forests to act as natural barriers that absorb storm surge energy.

Elevated Community Shelters Constructing raised, reinforced cyclone shelters in flat delta townships where no natural high ground exists.

Clear Public Warning Education Pairing weather forecasts with plain-language instructions explaining storm surge height and where to evacuate immediately.

Stay Safe During the Storm

Remember these three priorities when severe weather reaches your area.

01. Seek Shelter

Stay inside a sturdy building or move to a cyclone shelter.

02. Stay Away From Windows

Move to an interior room away from windows and glass doors.

03. Avoid Floodwater

Never walk, swim or drive through floodwater. Move to higher ground if water begins rising.

Different Situations

What you should do depends on where you are when the storm arrives.

If You're Indoors

Stay in a sturdy part of the building and protect yourself from broken glass and debris.

  • Stay in an interior room away from windows and glass doors.
  • Keep listening to official storm updates by radio or phone.
  • Follow evacuation instruction by authorities.
  • During lightning, avoid plugged-in electrical devices such as showers.

If You're Outdoors

Get to an enclosed building as soon as possible. Do not remain in open or exposed areas.

  • Stay away from beaches, the sea, rivers and areas that may flood.
  • Avoid trees, power lines, metal fences and damaged structures.
  • If you hear thunder, seek shelter in a building or fully enclosed vehicle.

If You're in a Vehicle

Avoid travelling during severe weather. Flooded roads and fallen trees can make routes dangerous.

  • Do not drive into floodwater, even if the road seems shallow.
  • If conditions become dangerous, stop in a safe place away from trees, power lines and flooded areas.
  • Do not park beneath trees or beside damaged power lines.

Wait for Everything to Clear

Even when the weather seems calmer, hazards can still remain. Do not go outside or return to an evacuated area until local authorities say it is safe.

  • Continue listening to official weather and safety updates.
  • Keep away from damaged buildings, fallen trees and loose debris.
  • Stay far away from fallen or dangling electrical wires.

Contact Family and Stay Informed

Let family members and trusted contacts know that you are safe. Communication networks may be disrupted, so send a short text message when calls cannot connect.
